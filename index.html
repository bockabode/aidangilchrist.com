Designing My Own RC Airplane

For the past few months I've been designing my own RC airplane in Onshape to 3D-print and fly. I finished the main design over the summer and have spent my free time since refining it.

  • Used online CFD simulations and Onshape's wing profile generators to find an airfoil that produces enough lift for the projected weight of about 700 g.
  • Ran acceleration and balance simulations natively in Onshape.
  • Lightening the wings and designing internal structural supports to hit that weight target.
  • Next up: a spar across the wings and a single-prop setup, hopefully finished before the new year.

Computational Physics in Python

For my final project in Physics last year, I chose to calculate the optimal launch angle for a baseball home run. I've played baseball for years and knew how the ball moves, but not exactly why, so I taught myself Python by building a tool that predicts how far a baseball travels. It's accurate to within about 5–10%, and I'm now adding the Magnus effect (spin) and altitude-dependent air density.

The code is on GitHub.

FRC 4131 · Liberty Robotics

I design and build competition robots with Liberty Robotics. My work includes CAD, drivetrain and climber design, and mechanisms such as adjustable hoods and indexing systems.

Last season I led the design analysis of a flip-climb mechanism to reach the highest (level 3) climb. Starting from Penn State's Robot in 3 Days flip climb, I learned about stress, strain, and material properties like Young's modulus to calculate the forces involved from our robot's weight and weight distribution, and worked out which materials and bolts it would take. It came out to roughly 4,800 N·m of torque to flip our 135 lb robot, so we decided not to build it, but it was one of the most useful things I learned all season.
